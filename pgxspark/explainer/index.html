Start here

Same drug, same dose, a very different outcome.

Carbamazepine is a common epilepsy medicine. In a small number of patients it triggers Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) or toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN): severe reactions where the skin blisters and peels.

A Malaysian study found one gene variant, HLA-B*1502, in 75% of Malay patients who had this reaction, but in only 15.7% of people who had not. (Chang et al., 2011)

A blood test can find that variant before the first tablet. Using a patient's genes to guide their medicines is called pharmacogenomics (PGx).

Read it · CYP2C19

Each allele has a function.

CYP2C19 has many known alleles. Four cover most results you will see in practice, and each is assigned a function: (CPIC, 2022)

AlleleFunctionEffect on the enzyme
*1NormalThe reference version; works as expected
*2, *3No functionMakes no working enzyme
*17IncreasedMakes more enzyme activity than *1

Everyone has two copies of CYP2C19, one from each parent. So a report gives a pair, such as *1/*2: one normal copy and one no-function copy. The order does not matter: *1/*2 and *2/*1 are the same result.

Read it · phenotypes

Two alleles, five phenotypes.

PhenotypeAllelesExamples
Ultrarapid (UM)Two increased*17/*17
Rapid (RM)One normal + one increased*1/*17
Normal (NM)Two normal*1/*1
Intermediate (IM)One no-function + one normal or increased*1/*2, *1/*3, *2/*17
Poor (PM)Two no-function*2/*2, *2/*3, *3/*3
Watch out: a *17 does not cancel out a no-function allele. CPIC classes *2/*17 as intermediate, not normal. (CPIC, 2022)

Reason · the key idea

Same enzyme, opposite consequence.

Most drugs are already active. An enzyme clears them, so a poor metaboliser keeps more drug in the blood.

A prodrug is inactive until an enzyme switches it on. Clopidogrel is a prodrug, and CYP2C19 is the enzyme that activates it. So the arrow flips: a poor metaboliser makes less active drug. (CPIC, 2022)

Decide · CYP2C19 and clopidogrel

Too little activation means too little protection.

Clopidogrel stops platelets clumping, which protects patients from another heart attack or a blocked stent. Intermediate and poor metabolisers get weaker platelet inhibition and a higher risk of major cardiovascular events. (CPIC, 2022)

PhenotypeCPIC recommendation after a heart attack or stent (ACS/PCI)
UM, RM, NMStandard-dose clopidogrel (75 mg a day)
IM, PMAvoid clopidogrel if possible. Use prasugrel or ticagrelor at standard dose if there is no contraindication.

Raising the clopidogrel dose is not the CPIC recommendation for IM or PM: it advises switching drug. Prasugrel and ticagrelor do not depend on CYP2C19 in the same way. (PharmGKB)

Reason · HLA-B and carbamazepine

Not a dose problem: an immune one.

HLA-B helps the immune system decide what to attack. In people who carry HLA-B*15:02, carbamazepine can trigger an immune attack on the skin, causing SJS/TEN. (CPIC, 2013)

Positive or negative
One or two copies of *15:02 is reported as positive. No copies is negative. There is no "intermediate" result.
Dose does not fix it
The allele is not known to change how carbamazepine is handled by the body. A lower dose does not remove the risk.

The US drug label says patients with ancestry in populations where HLA-B*15:02 may be present should be tested before starting carbamazepine. (Medical Genetics Summaries) The allele is common in parts of Asia, and the Malaysian study found it in Malay, Chinese and Indian patients who had the reaction. (Chang et al., 2011)

Decide · HLA-B*15:02 and carbamazepine

The result, and the history.

The decision depends on two things: the test result, and whether the patient has already been taking the drug. (CPIC, 2017)

ResultHistoryCPIC recommendation
NegativeAnyUse at usual dosing
PositiveNever taken itDo not use carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine
PositiveTaken consistently for over 3 months, no skin reactionCautiously consider continuing
A negative result lowers the risk but does not remove all of it. Every patient starting carbamazepine should be told to report any new rash.

QUESTION 2 OF 3
A positive patient has never taken carbamazepine. Would starting at half the usual dose make it safe?
No. The reaction is immune-mediated, not dose-related. CPIC says do not use carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine in this patient.

Cautiously consider continuing. After more than 3 months of consistent use without a skin reaction, CPIC allows cautious continuation.

Check your understanding

Five questions, no marks recorded.

QUESTION 1 OF 5
A patient's CYP2C19 result is *1/*2. What is the phenotype?
Intermediate: one normal and one no-function allele. Poor needs two no-function alleles.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
A CYP2C19 poor metaboliser takes standard-dose clopidogrel. What is the main concern?
Weaker platelet inhibition. "Builds up" is the prodrug trap: clopidogrel needs CYP2C19 to become active.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
A patient carries one copy of HLA-B*15:02. How is the result reported?
Positive. One or two copies both count; there is no intermediate result.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
HLA-B*15:02 positive, never taken carbamazepine. What does CPIC recommend?
Do not use carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine. A lower dose does not remove an immune-mediated risk.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
CYP2C19 *1/*17 (rapid metaboliser) after a stent. What does CPIC recommend?
Standard-dose clopidogrel. CPIC does not link increased activation with higher bleeding risk, so no dose change is needed.
Take home

Read, reason, then decide.

1
A report gives a pair of alleles. Their functions decide the phenotype, and the phenotype drives the advice.
2
For a prodrug like clopidogrel, a poor metaboliser gets less effect. CPIC advises prasugrel or ticagrelor for IM and PM after ACS or PCI.
3
HLA-B*15:02 risk is immune-mediated, so dose changes do not fix it. Positive and never exposed: avoid carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine.
