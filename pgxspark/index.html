From gene to decision.

Gene, allele, diplotype and phenotype. Why a poor metaboliser gets less effect from a prodrug like clopidogrel. Why HLA-B*15:02 risk is immune, not dose. Gated checks and a quiz on every idea.

02
Lab · genotype to decision

Read the report. Make the call.

A new patient every round. Tag each allele, assign the phenotype, predict the effect, run the simulation, then decide. Wrong reasoning is named, like the "prodrug trap", so the mistake teaches.

Try the lab →
03
Simulator · PGx Shift

One morning. Three patients. No alerts.

Work the outpatient counter as the pharmacist. Choose from the queue, check records, hold or dispense, phone the prescriber, counsel the patient. The shift ends with a report on every patient's outcome.

This shows adoption, not yet learning. Across 51 randomised trials, virtual patients improved skills more than traditional teaching and matched it for knowledge, on low-certainty evidence. Testing that at KPJ is the evaluation we propose.

A ladder, not a single tool

Three tiers of simulation.

Tier 1

Explain and practise

Paged explainers and step-checked labs, used across three pharmacy courses.

Piloted
Tier 2

Branching simulation

First-person shift simulators where decisions have outcomes. PGx Shift is the first.

Prototype, tested
Tier 3

Immersive simulation

Browser-based WebXR scenes for hands-on technique, such as aseptic handling or a QC laboratory.

Concept
What it could become

Built, not bought.

Commercial libraries already sell this model. Virtuosi, for pharmaceutical manufacturing, offers 56 on-demand courses and 20 VR simulations, licensed from a vendor.

KPJ already has the beginning of its own: owned in-house, built on KPJ's guidelines and local evidence, and growing with each new module. That is a direction, not a claim of equivalence. Today the library is a fraction of that scale.
